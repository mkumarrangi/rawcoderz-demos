## How the copy works

Every message follows the same four beats, which is why it stays short:

1. **Name the problem they already feel.** Not "you need a website", but the specific loss: the queue, the commission, the waiter walking to the lawn, the custom order taken wrong.
2. **Show the proof.** A finished sample site with their name on it, already live. That is the whole advantage, so the link goes high in the message.
3. **Stay honest.** Sample menu and prices, no real orders. This protects you and it reads as confidence, not weakness.
4. **Ask small.** Ten minutes, not a meeting, not a proposal.

Two things never to say, because neither is established and both will lose you the room: that the business has no website, and any promise of a specific sales lift or queue reduction.

## Sending order and timing

Work top to bottom. Batch 1 first, because those venues have the clearest gap and the shortest decision chain, usually one owner. Batch 3 last, because those are brands and groups where you have to find the right person before the message matters at all.

Send between 3pm and 6pm. Lunch service is over, dinner has not started, and the owner is actually looking at the phone. Avoid 12pm to 3pm and after 8pm.

Cap it at six to eight messages a day from one number, and edit the text per venue, so WhatsApp does not read it as bulk sending.

## If they reply

The demo has already done the selling, so the call is only about two questions. First, what actually breaks on their busiest day: the queue, the wrong orders, the phone ringing during service, or the aggregator commission. Second, who decides and what they spend today. Until you have both answers, do not quote a price and do not build anything custom.

When they ask what it costs, the honest answer is that it depends on whether they want the menu only or ordering connected to their kitchen, and that the sample they just opened is free either way.

## Follow up twice, then stop

Follow up on day 3 and day 8, then stop. Two follow-ups is persistence and a third is a nuisance.

Day 3, short and on the same thread:

```
Hi, just checking you saw the sample site we built for <Brand>:
<demo link>

Happy to leave it there if the timing is wrong. Would 10 minutes this week help?
```
